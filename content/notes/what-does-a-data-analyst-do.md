By the time I landed my first data role, reporting tools like Google Analytics and Data Studio had already been widely available. This meant reporting alone was the least important part of the job. The more important questions that surfaced were usually:

- "What actions are valuable?"
- “Who are likely to perform this action?”
- “How can we measure more efficiently?”
- “How can the company use this data?”

### **None of these questions made me think of dashboards as the solution. At least, not the main solution.**

They all called for statistics/modelling and engineering. The data analyst's role, in contrast to the data scientist's and data engineer's, has gained a reputation for focusing on visualisation; however, this role has begun to absorb more of the other disciplines. Statistics unearths new information, analytics engineering optimises information processing, software engineering enables a wider range of solutions, and agentic engineering brings the data and insights closer to stakeholders. My work as a Senior Data Analyst did all of that, and it's not too dissimilar from what the market demands from this career path. 

### **We just know better now.**

My former digital marketing career also moved in a way that consolidated multiple roles into one. By the time I moved to data, that career path had combined the jobs of a digital analyst (tracking and measurement), a marketer (campaign management), and a creative (content creation). I once thought it required too many roles; in retrospect, it was a singular role that built value using its own toolbox.

I now view the data profession the same way. It's better to focus on how each role achieves business value than excelling at each one. 

### **Dashboards as syntax**

I remember someone mentioning LLMs were good at coding because it's structured text and that it usually followed some form of syntax. One can argue that visualisation is still involved in these tasks as a form of discovery, simulation, presentation, or monitoring. Visualisation makes exploring datasets and displaying trends easy. Their interactive elements output values based on user input to demonstrate cases. Dashboards also function as easily shareable presentation material. Because dashboards typically use regularly updated data, they often serve as accessible monitoring tools.
